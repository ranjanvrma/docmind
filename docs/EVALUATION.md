# Evaluation

A system that produces fluent answers is not necessarily a good one. DocMind measures **retrieval** (did the right page come back?) separately from **answers** (was the answer correct and grounded?).

Code: `evaluation/evaluate.py` (command line), `POST /api/evaluation/retrieval` (the Settings page's *Evaluation lab*). Both use the same function, `run_retrieval_evaluation`, and index the dataset's PDFs into a **temporary** directory, so the real index is never touched.

## Dataset

`evaluation/datasets/sample_eval.json`: a **sample/demo dataset**.

- 3 short fictional PDFs (`evaluation/sample_docs/`, generated by `make_sample_docs.py`): a remote-work policy, a solar-microgrid report and ML lecture notes; 9 pages in total.
- 20 retrieval queries, each labelled with the `(document, page)` pairs that contain the answer.
- 9 QA items with reference answers, 3 of them deliberately unanswerable.
- Written by the project author, who also wrote the documents.

Relevance is labelled at **page** level, because page labels stay valid when chunk size or overlap changes; chunk IDs would not.

## Retrieval metrics

For each query, the top max(K) chunks are retrieved; a chunk is relevant if it comes from a labelled page.

| Metric | Definition | Code |
|---|---|---|
| Hit@K (Top-K hit rate) | 1 if any of the top K is relevant | `hit_at_k` |
| Precision@K | relevant chunks in top K ÷ K | `precision_at_k` |
| Recall@K | distinct relevant pages found in top K ÷ relevant pages | `recall_at_k` |
| MRR | mean of 1 / rank of the first relevant chunk | `reciprocal_rank` |

## Measured results (demo dataset only)

Model `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`. Measured with `python evaluation/evaluate.py` and in the Evaluation lab:

| Setting | Hit@1 | Hit@3 | Hit@5 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chunk 800 / overlap 150 (default) | 0.70 | 0.95 | 1.00 | 0.838 |
| chunk 600 / overlap 150 | 0.85 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.92 |

Precision@5 at the default is 0.24. That is expected, not a defect: most queries have a single relevant page among 11 chunks, so at least four of five results must be irrelevant.

**Why these numbers are not evidence of real-world quality**
- 20 queries: one query changes Hit@1 by 5 points; the difference between the two settings above is 3 queries.
- The same person wrote the documents and the questions, so the wording overlaps unrealistically.
- With only 11–18 chunks, even random ordering would often hit within the top 5.
- Clean, single-column synthetic text; no tables, scans or long documents.
- No baseline (such as BM25) and no held-out split; labels were not independently checked.

Use the lab to **compare settings**, then confirm on real data.

## Qualitative observations

- Rank-1 misses at the default were mostly vocabulary mismatches ("online" vs "reachable", "memorising" vs "overfits").
- Retrieval scores cannot separate answerable from unanswerable questions: "parental leave policy" (not in any document) retrieved a passage scoring 0.52, higher than some correct answers. The "not found" behaviour therefore depends on the LLM.

## Answer (QA) evaluation

`python evaluation/evaluate.py --qa` (requires an LLM) records, per question:
- **token F1** vs the reference answer (lexical overlap; crude);
- **false-abstention rate** on answerable questions and **correct-abstention rate** on unanswerable ones;
- **context recall**: did a relevant page reach the prompt? This separates retrieval failures from generation failures;
- **citation precision**: cited sources that come from relevant pages;
- **invalid citations**.

**Status: the QA evaluation has not been run.** A live LLM was spot-checked by hand (see [LLM_INTEGRATION.md](LLM_INTEGRATION.md#verification-status)), but no QA metrics are claimed.

## Better methodology for real use

1. Real documents of the target kind (tables, multi-column layouts, long reports).
2. 100+ questions written by people who did not write the documents, including unanswerable ones.
3. Relevance labelled by two annotators, with agreement measured.
4. Baselines (BM25, random) and confidence intervals (e.g. bootstrap).
5. Tune on a development split; report on a held-out test split.
6. For answers: human, or human-validated LLM-judge, ratings of faithfulness per claim and correctness per answer.
